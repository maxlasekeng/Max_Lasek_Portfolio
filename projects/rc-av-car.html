RC / Autonomous Car

Jun 2026 to presentIn progress

A tank-drive remote control and autonomous vehicle that navigates between coordinates using PID control, differential-drive odometry, and a Raspberry Pi 5.

Why I built it

I wanted to expand my skills in electronics and embedded systems, and a small car that is programmable and expandable was the best way to start learning.

How it works

The car uses a tank-drive layout with free-swiveling front casters, and steers by varying the speed of its two rear wheels. Wheel encoders feed an odometry calculation that tracks position and heading in real time. Two cascaded PID loops handle navigation: an outer loop steers toward each waypoint, and an inner loop sets each wheel’s throttle.

Power

A 2S LiPo feeds an XT60 splitter. One branch runs through a buck converter that regulates 5 V for the Pi. The other sends the raw 7.4 to 8.4 V to the motor HAT, which stacks on the Pi and drives the two DC motors through soldered leads.

Where it stands

I designed the car, selected all the parts, soldered the wiring to the Pi HAT, and assembled everything. The car drives forward and stops on command. I’m now tuning the PID loops to remove overshoot when it drives a set distance.

Along the way I ran FEA on several TPU wheel designs to compare how different shapes deform under load, and rearranged the electronics into a compact, weight-balanced layout with the battery mounted low.

Key decisions

I used a Raspberry Pi instead of an Arduino to challenge myself and to make the project easier to expand. I left room for a second deck, and printed the wheels in TPU for flexibility.

Alternatives I considered

Servo steering

A servo with an encoder would turn the front wheels instead of tank drive. I didn’t have the parts or the time to order them, so I switched to swivel casters.

Arduino

An Arduino would suit a simple version of this project. I didn’t have a Bluetooth module for it, and it is less capable than the Pi for adding features like a camera later.

Next iterations

Finish the code and tune the car accurately, then control it with a Bluetooth controller. After that: better wire management, a stronger connection for the Pi HAT, a second deck for a camera, and lower infill on the TPU wheels for more flexibility.

Electrical components

  • 2S 7.4 V 2200 mAh LiPo battery
  • 2× DC motors with encoders
